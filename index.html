About Me

I am a Ph.D. student in Applied Economics at the University of Florida (Advisor: Dr. Zhengfei Guan) and an M.S. student in Applied Mathematics at Johns Hopkins University (Advisor: Prof. James Spall). Prior to this, I completed a Certificate in International Studies at Hopkins Nanjing Center and an M.Phil. in Economics at Wuhan University.

My research lies at the intersection of technology, environment, and economic development. I develop theoretical models and empirical strategies to understand how AI, automation, and digital technologies reshape labor markets, ecological footprints, and growth dynamics — particularly in transforming and developing economies.

I have published in journals including Ecological Economics, Environmental and Resource Economics, Review of Development Economics, Journal of Regional Science, and Energy Economics, with over 428 citations on Google Scholar.

Current Research Projects
Phillips Curve in Dual Economy
Develops a dual-economy equilibrium model showing how inflation reduces capital accumulation and leads to a positive inflation–unemployment relationship in developing countries.
AI and Corporate ESG Strategy Facing Extreme Weather
Explores how companies adjust ESG strategies in response to extreme weather events and the role AI technology plays in this adaptation, using large-scale daily weather data.
Substitution Effects of AI on Labor
Constructs a general equilibrium model examining how AI impacts labor substitution across industries under varying conditions of capital abundance, urbanization, and industrialization.
